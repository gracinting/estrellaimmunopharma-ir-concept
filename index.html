Estrella Immunopharma · Nasdaq: ESLA

A clinical-stage T-cell therapy company

Estrella develops T-cell therapies built on the ARTEMIS receptor platform. Its lead candidate, EB103, targets CD19 on B cells and is in STARLIGHT-1, a Phase I/II trial in relapsed or refractory B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma, where the company reports that Phase II dosing began in January 2026. The company’s own filings also state substantial doubt about its ability to continue as a going concern. 10-K, Item 1 10-Q, Note 1

The science

Two receptors, one T cell: how ARTEMIS is meant to work

Estrella licenses the ARTEMIS platform from its parent, Eureka Therapeutics. The 10-K describes it as an Antibody-T Cell Receptor (AbTCR) design intended to address limits of conventional CAR T-cell therapy, such as toxicity and exhaustion. 10-K, Item 1

  1. 1

    The target is CD19

    EB103 is a CD19-directed therapy. The 10-K says CD19, like CD22, is expressed on the surface of most B-cell malignancies; the receptors are built to recognize and bind it. 10-K, Item 1

  2. 2

    The AbTCR binds it

    The AbTCR is the first of two receptors the engineered T cell carries. Its effector domain is made of γδ T-cell receptor chains. 10-K, Item 1

  3. 3

    The T cell’s own CD3 answers

    Those chains associate with the T cell’s endogenous CD3 complex. When the AbTCR binds its target, signalling through the AbTCR/CD3 complex begins and activates the T cell. 10-K, Item 1

  4. 4

    A second, separate signal

    A co-stimulatory molecule on the same T cell also binds CD19 and generates a second “enhancement” signal. The company calls it a differentiating feature that this molecule is a separate protein, not linked to the activation domain. 10-K, Item 1

What the company says is different, and what it has shown so far

These are the 10-K’s statements of belief. They rest on Eureka’s internal and published studies (the Phase I results are reported separately, in the next section); the 10-K’s own risk factors say clinical trials have uncertain outcomes and that preliminary data may change. 10-K, Item 1 10-K, Item 1A

  • Lower inflammatory signalling. In comparisons with market-standard CAR-T constructs the company says ARTEMIS T cells showed comparable or better antitumor potency with significantly lower release of inflammatory cytokines such as IL-6, which drive cytokine release syndrome. It adds that it believes this has the potential to allow administration outside dedicated cancer centers.
  • Function at low antigen density. Citing 2025 National Cancer Institute studies on a different target (GPC2, in solid tumors), the company says ARTEMIS T cells kept working at antigen levels below those that activate conventional CAR-T cells.
  • More stem-like memory cells, fewer exhaustion markers. Preclinical data, the company says, point to expansion of stem-like memory T cells and fewer exhaustion markers than standard CAR-T constructs; it adds that this subset has been reported to be associated with long-term persistence and durability of clinical response.

The trial

STARLIGHT-1: nine Phase I patients, and the schedule that pays for the trial

An open-label, dose-escalation, multi-center Phase I/II trial of EB103 in adults with relapsed or refractory B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NCT06343311). The FDA cleared the IND on Mar 2, 2023; the first patient was dosed in July 2024. 10-K, Item 1

Who was treated
Nine patients in two dose levels: three at 2.5 million and six at 5 million receptor-positive T cells per kilogram. The median was three prior lines of therapy; the company counts about 80% as high-risk, 33% had a prior autologous stem cell transplant and 89% had two or more prior regimens.
Safety, as reported
No treatment-related serious adverse events. Cytokine release syndrome occurred in all treated patients, always Grade 1 or 2, with no Grade 3 or higher at either dose. A majority had transient low-grade ICANS, with a median duration of two days.
Response, as reported
At the higher dose, 5 of 5 evaluable patients (100%) had a complete response at Month 1. One more patient in that group had a Grade 5 (fatal) sepsis event on Day 18, which the investigator judged unrelated to EB103; that patient was not evaluable for response. All evaluable patients remained in remission at the Jan 28, 2026 data cutoff. The 10-K reports a response rate only for the higher-dose group, and cautions that these results are based on a small number of patients and limited follow-up and may not be predictive of results in larger populations, additional cohorts or later-stage trials.
What came next
An independent Data Safety Monitoring Board reviewed the Phase I safety data in December 2025 and recommended moving to Phase II at the recommended dose. Phase II dosing began in January 2026 “to confirm the Phase I results,” and the 10-K (March 2026) says the company anticipates completing the Phase II portion in the first half of 2027. The 10-K does not tie that date to funding; separately, management says it will not have sufficient funds to meet its working capital requirements and debt obligations as they become due, starting from one year from the date of the 10-Q (see Disclosures).

Disclosures

What the company’s own filings say about its position

Drawn from the going-concern notes, the risk factors and the Nasdaq notices in the 10-K, the 10-Q and the 8-Ks. This is a selection, not the whole; the filings are the whole.

  1. 01

    Substantial doubt about continuing as a going concern

    The 10-Q for the quarter ended Jun 30, 2026 says management has determined there is substantial doubt about the company’s ability to continue as a going concern, citing four things: recurring losses (about $4.3M in the first half of 2026), an accumulated deficit of about $41.6M, cash of about $0.1M against current liabilities of about $9.3M including about $8.3M of accrued milestone obligations payable to Eureka, and net cash used in operations of about $8.5M. The auditor’s report in the 10-K for 2025 carries a going-concern paragraph too. 10-Q, Note 1 10-K, Item 8

    “Management is of the opinion that we will not have sufficient funds to meet our working capital requirements and debt obligations as they become due starting from one year from the date of this report. If we are unable to obtain adequate financing or generate significant revenue, we may be required to curtail or cease our operations.” 10-Q, MD&A

    The company says it plans to raise additional capital but that there is no assurance financing will be available on acceptable terms, or at all. The only facility the filings name, a common stock purchase agreement with White Lion Capital LLC, expired on Dec 30, 2025 and is no longer a source of liquidity. 10-Q, MD&A

  2. 02

    Eureka is the company’s licensor, supplier, service provider, creditor and controlling stockholder

    Eureka Therapeutics owned about 58.7% of the company’s outstanding shares at Jun 30, 2026 (66.6% at Dec 31, 2025; the 10-Q attributes the decrease mainly to dilution from the January 2026 financing). The 10-K says Eureka holds more than 50% of the voting power, so the company is a “controlled company” under Nasdaq rules, and that this may limit other stockholders’ ability to influence corporate matters. 10-Q, Note 5 10-K, Item 1A Its summary of risk factors says the company is heavily dependent on Eureka for manufacturing (as sole supplier of clinical drug product), research and development support and information technology, and that termination or disruption of those services “would severely impact our operations”; Item 1A adds that if the arrangements end or are disrupted the company would not have an immediately available alternative source of supply. 10-K, Risk factor summary 10-K, Item 1A

    The company’s chief executive officer, president and director, Dr. Liu, is also the chief executive officer and president of Eureka and a beneficial owner of more than 10% of its equity, and the 10-K says he therefore devotes less than full time to the company’s business; it lists such positions and equity interests among actual or potential conflicts of interest. The auditor’s report carries an emphasis-of-matter paragraph on significant transactions with, and supplier concentration in, two related parties. 10-K, Item 1A 10-K, Item 8

    The money runs the same way. ARTEMIS is licensed from Eureka ($1.0M paid; up to 5 development milestones of about $60.2M in all, 4 sales milestones totalling $225.0M, and royalties of a single-digit percentage of net sales), and the trial is run under the statement of work described above. At Jun 30, 2026 the company had accrued about $8.3M to related parties, which the 10-Q describes as earned but not yet formally invoiced milestone payments owed to Eureka; $54,999 of that liability is a consulting accrual owed to another related party. The statement of work also lets Eureka invoice pass-through costs, which the filings do not quantify. Other related-party arrangements include a consulting agreement with a firm managed by director Hong Zhang (terminated effective Jul 1, 2026; $109,998 of expense in the first half of 2026, and 520,858 unvested shares forfeited) and a $2,000-a-month office sublease from Eureka, renewed to Dec 31, 2026. 10-K, Item 1 10-Q, Note 5 10-Q, Note 11

  4. 04

    Raising money will dilute existing holders

    At Jun 30, 2026 the company had 43,034,228 shares outstanding and 13,409,928 shares of potential common stock: public warrants for 2,214,993 at $11.50, common warrants for 7,594,935 at $1.39 and 3,600,000 options. A further 463,636 true-up shares were issued on Jul 1, 2026; the 2025 purchase agreements require additional shares if the stock price on the 12-month anniversary of each closing is below $1.50, up to 735,857 in all, which leaves up to 272,221 more (calculated). The 10-Q says the public warrants are unlikely to be exercised while the share price ($0.722 on Aug 10, 2026) is far below their $11.50 exercise price. Any new equity financing would add to the share count. 10-Q, Note 2 10-Q, Note 7 10-Q, MD&A

  5. 05

    The clinical evidence is early and small

    The company has no approved product and no revenue. Its Phase I data cover nine patients; the 10-K warns that interim, topline and preliminary data may change as more data becomes available, that clinical trials involve uncertain outcomes, and that regulatory approval is lengthy and unpredictable. EB104 and EB201 are not yet in the clinic, and the 10-K gives no timetable for the solid-tumor concept. The 10-K’s anticipated completion of the Phase II portion in the first half of 2027 is a forward-looking statement; the 10-K does not tie that date to funding, and item 01 sets out management’s funding conclusion. 10-K, Item 1 10-K, Item 1A

  6. 06

    Material weaknesses in internal control

    The 10-K concludes that internal control over financial reporting was not effective at Dec 31, 2025 because of a lack of qualified full-time personnel with appropriate accounting knowledge and experience to address complex U.S. GAAP issues, and the 10-Q concludes that disclosure controls and procedures were not effective at Jun 30, 2026 because of the material weakness; the 10-K adds that if it is not remediated the reliability of the financial statements could be affected. 10-K, Item 9A 10-Q, Item 4 10-K, Item 1A
